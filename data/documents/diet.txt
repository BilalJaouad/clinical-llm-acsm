A heart-healthy diet generally emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and sources of unsaturated fat such as olive oil and fish. These patterns are commonly grouped under names like the Mediterranean diet or the DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet.

Reducing sodium (salt) intake is one of the most studied dietary changes for supporting healthy blood pressure. Processed and packaged foods are often the largest source of dietary sodium, more so than salt added at the table.

Saturated and trans fats, found in many fried foods, pastries, and fatty cuts of meat, are generally associated with higher LDL ("bad") cholesterol, a known cardiovascular risk factor. Replacing some of these fats with unsaturated fats is a commonly recommended approach.

Fiber-rich foods such as oats, beans, and vegetables can help support healthy cholesterol levels and digestion. Limiting added sugars and sugary drinks is also generally recommended for overall cardiovascular and metabolic health.

Dietary needs vary by individual, especially for people with existing heart conditions, diabetes, or kidney disease. A doctor, dietitian, or nutritionist can help tailor recommendations to a person's specific situation.
