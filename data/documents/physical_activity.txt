Regular physical activity is one of the most consistently recommended ways to support cardiovascular health. General public health guidelines often suggest around 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, such as brisk walking, cycling, or swimming.

Aerobic exercise can help the heart pump blood more efficiently, support healthy blood pressure and cholesterol levels, and help with weight management. Strength training a couple of times a week is also commonly recommended alongside aerobic activity.

For people who are inactive, new to exercise, or living with an existing heart condition, starting gradually and consulting a doctor before beginning a new exercise program is generally advised, since exercise intensity and type may need to be adapted.

Warning signs to stop exercising and seek medical attention include chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness, or irregular heartbeat during physical activity.

Even modest increases in daily movement, such as taking the stairs or short walks, can contribute to cardiovascular health, particularly for people who are currently very sedentary.
