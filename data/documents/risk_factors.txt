Cardiovascular risk factors are generally grouped into modifiable and non-modifiable categories. Non-modifiable factors include age, sex, and family history of heart disease.

Modifiable risk factors include high blood pressure, high LDL cholesterol, smoking, physical inactivity, an unhealthy diet, excess body weight, high alcohol consumption, and poorly managed diabetes or stress. Addressing several of these together often has a greater cumulative benefit than addressing just one.

Smoking is one of the most significant modifiable risk factors, damaging blood vessels and accelerating the buildup of arterial plaque. Risk tends to decrease over time after quitting, though the exact timeline varies between individuals.

Diabetes, and elevated blood sugar more generally, can damage blood vessels over time and is closely linked with cardiovascular disease risk, which is why blood sugar management is often considered alongside cardiovascular care.

Regular check-ups can help track blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, and weight over time, allowing risk factors to be identified and addressed earlier. A doctor can help interpret personal risk based on a person's full medical history.
