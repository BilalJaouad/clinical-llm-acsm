Hypertension, or high blood pressure, occurs when the force of blood against artery walls stays too high over time. It is usually defined as a reading at or above 140/90 mmHg, though target thresholds can vary by guideline and by individual health context.

Hypertension is often called a "silent" condition because it usually causes no obvious symptoms. Many people only discover it during a routine check-up, which is why regular blood pressure monitoring is recommended, especially for adults over 40 or those with a family history of heart disease.

Lifestyle factors commonly associated with high blood pressure include a diet high in salt, low physical activity, excess body weight, high alcohol intake, chronic stress, and smoking. Some cases are also linked to genetics or underlying kidney or hormonal conditions.

Left unmanaged over the long term, hypertension increases the risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney damage, and heart failure. Management generally combines lifestyle changes with, when prescribed by a doctor, medication tailored to the individual.

Anyone concerned about their blood pressure readings, or considering changes to a prescribed treatment, should discuss this with a doctor or pharmacist rather than adjusting medication on their own.
